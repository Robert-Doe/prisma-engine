Module 0 — ConceptSystematized Review

In one image A home-cooked meal made carefully, following a professional recipe as closely as one cook alone can — genuinely rigorous effort, but not the same as a restaurant kitchen with a full brigade checking every plate before it goes out.

A systematized review applies elements of systematic-review methodology — a documented search strategy, explicit eligibility criteria, sometimes quality appraisal — without meeting the full standard, most often because it's produced by a single reviewer (e.g. a student thesis or dissertation) rather than an independent-dual-reviewer team. Naming it honestly, rather than calling it "systematic," is the entire point of the category.

What's typically missing vs. a full systematic review

  • Independent dual screening (usually one reviewer does everything)
  • A registered, peer-reviewed protocol
  • Full-scale, multi-database search comprehensiveness
This is often the honest, correct label for course/thesis work. A single graduate student doing a careful, documented literature search and screen for a thesis chapter is very often producing a systematized review — and that's a legitimate, well-earned label, not a lesser one.
How this connects

A resource-constrained sibling of Systematic review. Compare Rapid review, which simplifies for a time constraint rather than a reviewer-count constraint — the two are often combined in practice.
