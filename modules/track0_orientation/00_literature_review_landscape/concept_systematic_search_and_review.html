Module 0 — ConceptSystematic Search and Review

In one image A detective who gathers every piece of evidence with rigorous, documented procedure — then explains the case to the jury as a story, in their own words, instead of reading the evidence log aloud.

A systematic search and review uses a systematic, reproducible search strategy (comparable rigor to a full systematic review) but then applies a narrative, non-systematic synthesis to what was found — combining the reproducibility of one half of the process with the interpretive flexibility of the other.

What makes it distinct

  • Search methodology: systematic — multiple databases, documented strategy, reproducible
  • Synthesis methodology: narrative — author judgment, not protocol-driven pooling or formal thematic synthesis
  • Common when the search needs defending (e.g. in a grant proposal's background section) but full systematic synthesis isn't the goal
Why name this as its own category: without it, authors who did a rigorous search but a narrative synthesis tend to either overclaim "systematic review" (misleading — the synthesis wasn't systematic) or underclaim "narrative review" (misleading the other way — undersells how rigorous the search actually was). Naming the hybrid honestly avoids both.
How this connects

Half of Systematic review's methodology (the search half) combined with Narrative review's synthesis approach. Compare Systematized review, which simplifies for a different reason (limited resources, not a deliberate synthesis choice).
