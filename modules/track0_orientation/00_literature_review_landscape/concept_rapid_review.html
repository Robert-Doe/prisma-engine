Module 0 — ConceptRapid Review

In one image Field triage instead of a full hospital workup. Same underlying medical judgment, deliberately compressed because a decision is needed now — and the chart notes exactly which steps got skipped, so nobody downstream mistakes it for the full workup.

A rapid review applies systematic-review methodology under a hard time constraint (often 1–6 weeks instead of 6–18 months), by deliberately simplifying specific steps: a single reviewer instead of two independent ones, fewer databases searched, a narrower date range, or lighter quality appraisal. The defining discipline is not the shortcuts themselves — it's documenting exactly which ones were taken.

Typical simplifications

  • Single-reviewer screening instead of dual independent screening
  • Restricted source list (e.g. 2 databases instead of 6)
  • Limiting to English-language and/or recent publications only
  • Simplified or omitted formal risk-of-bias scoring
The trap: a rapid review that doesn't disclose its shortcuts reads to an unsuspecting audience as a full systematic review with unusually thin methods — worse than being upfront about doing a rapid review in the first place.
How this connects

A compressed sibling of Systematic review — same rigor target, different time budget. Contrast with Systematized review, which simplifies not because of a deadline but because of limited reviewer resources (often just one student).
