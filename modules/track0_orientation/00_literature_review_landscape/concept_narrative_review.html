Module 0 — ConceptTraditional / Narrative Review

In one image A tour guide, not a census-taker. They choose which streets to walk you down, in an order designed to build your understanding — and a good guide is judged by the quality of that judgment, not by whether they showed you literally every street in the city.

A narrative (traditional) review is an author-guided account of a topic, organized to build understanding, context, or argument — not to exhaustively and reproducibly account for all available evidence. It's the oldest and most common review type, and the default form of the "literature review" chapter in most theses.

What it is, and isn't

  • No requirement for a comprehensive, reproducible search — sources are selected by expert judgment
  • No fixed reporting standard equivalent to PRISMA
  • Its value comes from expert synthesis and framing, not from search completeness
Not a lesser systematic review: a narrative review isn't a systematic review done badly — it's a different tool, valued for exactly the selective, interpretive judgment a systematic review deliberately tries to remove. The failure mode is calling it "systematic" when it isn't, not the narrative approach itself.
How this connects

Closest sibling: Critical review — both are configurative and author-driven; critical review goes further, explicitly critiquing and reconceptualizing rather than just describing. The thesis literature-review chapter built in this course is a structured, disciplined form of narrative review.
