Module 0 — ConceptMixed Studies / Mixed Methods Review

In one image A trial with both an expert-witness statistician and a witness describing what it felt like to live through the event — the verdict has to account for both kinds of testimony, in their own terms, not force one into the other's format.

A mixed studies (or mixed methods) review systematically includes and synthesizes primary studies that used quantitative, qualitative, and mixed methods, keeping the full systematic-review search and appraisal rigor while using a synthesis approach able to combine numeric and narrative findings without forcing one into the other's shape.

What makes it distinct

  • Systematic search and screening (same rigor bar as a standard systematic review)
  • Separate, methodology-appropriate quality appraisal tools for quantitative vs. qualitative studies
  • A synthesis approach (e.g. convergent, sequential, or "following a thread") designed specifically to integrate the two data types
Distinguishing it from integrative review: mixed methods review keeps closer to systematic-review-level search and appraisal rigor; integrative review's boundaries are looser and can include theoretical/non-empirical works too. In casual use the two terms are often swapped anyway — check the actual methods, not the label.
