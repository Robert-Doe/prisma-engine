Module 0 — ConceptLiving Systematic Review

In one image A Wikipedia article vs. a printed encyclopedia entry. The printed version is correct the day it's bound and steadily goes stale after that; the living version is republished continuously as new evidence arrives, with a visible history of what changed and when.

A living systematic review keeps full systematic-review methodology (protocol, comprehensive search, appraisal, PRISMA-style reporting) but is maintained as an ongoing, versioned artifact instead of a one-time publication — periodically re-run against new literature, with each update clearly dated and diffable against the last.

What it requires beyond a standard systematic review

  • A defined update cadence (e.g. monthly, quarterly) rather than a single search date
  • Incremental processing — only new records since the last run need full screening, not the whole corpus again
  • Explicit versioning so a reader can see what conclusion held at any past point in time
When it's worth the overhead: fast-moving fields where a static review goes stale within months of publication (this was a major driver of living reviews during COVID-19 research, where new trials were published weekly).
How this connects

An operational mode of Systematic review, not a different synthesis method — same rigor, different lifecycle. Compare State-of-the-art review, which also updates periodically but without the systematic rigor.
