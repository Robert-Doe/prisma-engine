Module 0 — ConceptSystematic Review

In one image An armored truck: slow to build, expensive to run, but engineered so every single thing that happened to it — every stop, every package loaded or rejected — is on a manifest someone else can audit later.

A systematic review answers a narrow, focused question (typically framed as PICO) by exhaustively searching for all relevant evidence, screening it against pre-registered eligibility criteria, appraising its quality, and synthesizing the results — with the entire process logged well enough that another team could, in principle, reproduce it and reach the same set of included studies.

What makes it "systematic" specifically

  • A protocol written and often registered before searching begins
  • A comprehensive, reproducible search across multiple databases, not just one convenient source
  • Explicit, pre-specified eligibility criteria applied consistently (ideally by two independent reviewers)
  • Formal quality/risk-of-bias appraisal of every included study
  • PRISMA-compliant reporting, including the flow diagram
The tell: if you can't say how many databases were searched, what the exact eligibility criteria were, or how many records were excluded and why, it isn't a systematic review — whatever else it might be.
How this connects

Closest sibling: Meta-analysis is what you do with a systematic review's included studies when their outcomes are numerically poolable — a systematic review doesn't require a meta-analysis, but a meta-analysis requires the systematic search underneath it. Compare against Systematized review (same ambition, doesn't meet the full standard) and Rapid review (same standard, deliberately compressed).
