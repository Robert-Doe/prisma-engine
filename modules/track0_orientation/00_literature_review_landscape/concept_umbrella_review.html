Module 0 — ConceptUmbrella Review

In one image A review of restaurant critics' reviews, not of the restaurants themselves. It reads and evaluates other people's already-completed verdicts, rather than tasting the food directly.

An umbrella review treats other systematic reviews (and meta-analyses) as its unit of analysis, rather than primary studies. It's used when enough systematic reviews already exist on related questions that the most valuable synthesis is comparing and consolidating them — common in fields with many overlapping reviews of the same broad topic (e.g. dozens of reviews on different aspects of a common disease).

What makes it distinct

  • Search targets existing systematic reviews specifically, not primary research
  • Appraisal tools are built for reviews (e.g. AMSTAR-style checklists), not primary studies
  • A key output is often identifying where included reviews disagree, or where their evidence overlaps (citing the same primary studies more than once)
The recursive hazard: primary studies frequently get cited by multiple overlapping systematic reviews. An umbrella review that naively pools numbers across reviews risks double-counting the same underlying data — this is the main methodological hazard specific to this type.
How this connects

Depends on Systematic review as its raw material. Overlaps loosely with Overview, which also often summarizes existing reviews but without the same methodological rigor.
