Module 0 — ConceptMapping Review / Systematic Map

In one image A census, not a story. Instead of narrating what the terrain is like, a mapping review counts and codes every settlement on it — X studies used design A, Y studies covered population B — and draws the result as a chart.

A mapping review (also called a systematic map) systematically catalogs existing research by coding each study's characteristics — design, population, intervention, geography, year — to visualize where evidence is dense and where it's sparse. The deliverable is usually a literal visual matrix or chart, not primarily prose.

What distinguishes it from a scoping review

  • Heavier emphasis on structured coding of every study against a fixed set of categories
  • Output leans toward a database/visualization the reader can query, more than a narrative account
  • Often explicitly framed as identifying gaps for future primary research, not just for future reviews
In practice: "scoping review" and "mapping review" are used almost interchangeably by many authors — treat the distinction as a matter of emphasis, not a hard boundary. If in doubt, describe what you actually did rather than picking a label to fit.
