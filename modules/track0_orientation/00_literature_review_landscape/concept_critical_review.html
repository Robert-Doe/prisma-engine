Module 0 — ConceptCritical Review

In one image Not a tour guide describing the city as it stands, but an urban planner arguing the whole street grid is laid out wrong — and sketching a better one.

A critical review goes beyond describing what the existing literature says: it evaluates and critiques it, often to expose contradictions, weaknesses in prior conceptualizations, or gaps in how a field has framed its own questions — frequently producing a new conceptual model or framework as its output, rather than a synthesis of existing ones.

What makes it "critical" specifically

  • Explicit evaluative stance — it argues a position about the field, not just reports on it
  • Often reconceptualizes or reorganizes the field's own categories, rather than accepting them as given
  • Search is purposive and illustrative, not exhaustive — depth of argument matters more than coverage
Recognize it by its output: if a review's main contribution is a new framework, typology, or theoretical model — not a pooled finding or a map of the field — it's functioning as a critical review, whatever it's labeled.
How this connects

Shares its author-driven, configurative approach with Narrative review, but with an explicitly evaluative rather than descriptive stance. Compare Realist review, which is also explanatory but grounded in systematically extracted mechanism data rather than argument alone.

Not built as a standalone module in this course — its critical, argument-building instinct is closest to Module 28's gap-statement generation.
