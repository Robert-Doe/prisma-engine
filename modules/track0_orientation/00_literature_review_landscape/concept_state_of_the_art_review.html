Module 0 — ConceptState-of-the-Art Review

In one image A weather report, not a climate history. It cares intensely about right now and what's about to happen next — older conditions matter only as far as they explain the current front moving in.

A state-of-the-art review surveys the current, most recent understanding of a fast-moving topic, deliberately prioritizing recency over historical completeness. It's common in fast-moving technical fields (machine learning, systems research) where a review covering the last 20 years would bury the reader in obsolete approaches.

What distinguishes it

  • Explicit recency bias — older foundational work is summarized briefly, recent work gets the depth
  • Often organized by current sub-approach or technique family, not chronology
  • Frequently produced as a standing, periodically-updated document rather than a one-time snapshot
Where you've likely seen this: a conference paper's or textbook chapter's "recent advances in X" section is almost always functioning as a compressed state-of-the-art review.
How this connects

Shares its broad, configurative, narrative-leaning approach with Narrative review and Overview — the distinguishing feature is purely the recency filter. Its periodic-update instinct foreshadows Living systematic review, though that type keeps full systematic rigor while this one doesn't.
