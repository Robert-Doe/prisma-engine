Module 0 — ConceptScoping Review

In one image A scout on a motorbike, sent ahead to report back what terrain exists — where the forests are, where the rivers cross — without stopping to fight, or even fully assess, everything along the way.

A scoping review maps the breadth of a field: what kinds of studies exist, what populations and outcomes they cover, what methods have been used, and — often the most valuable output — where the gaps are. It answers "what does this field look like?" rather than "what is the effect of X?"

How it differs mechanically from a systematic review

  • Eligibility criteria are broader and about coverage, not narrow effectiveness questions
  • No formal risk-of-bias/quality appraisal is usually done — the goal is charting existence, not judging trustworthiness
  • Output is typically a narrative account plus a charted/tabulated summary of study characteristics, not a pooled effect
When to reach for this instead of a systematic review: when you don't yet know if there's enough evidence to ask a narrower question — scoping reviews are often done first, specifically to justify (or rule out) doing a full systematic review afterward.
How this connects

Nearly identical methodology to Mapping review — the distinction is emphasis: scoping leans narrative, mapping leans toward a coded, visual catalog. Both sit far from Systematic review on the aggregative axis, since neither pools an effect.
