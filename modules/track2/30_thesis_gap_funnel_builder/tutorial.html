00Before You Start

Finished Module 29 — this module picks up exactly where that one's coverage count left off.

01The Big Analogy

An actual funnel Pour a whole bucket of water at the wide top of a funnel and it has to narrow, forced by the funnel's own shape, until it exits as one thin stream that fits through a single bottle's neck. Nobody has to convince the water to narrow — the shape does it.

build_funnel() is that shape. Stage 1 is the whole bucket — every theme the literature covers. Stage 2 narrows using a real measurement (which themes are thin). Stage 3 is the single stream — one specific gap. And then formalize_thesis_question() pours that stream through the exact bottle-neck Module 1 built at the very start of this course: a validated, PICO-structured research question.

02What Happens, Step by Step

  1. identify_thin_themes() reuses Module 29's coverage() directly — any theme with only threshold sources or fewer is thin
  2. build_funnel() produces three stages: broad context (every theme, named), narrowing (which themes are thin, with the real count), and gap (the single thinnest theme, named as the focus)
  3. formalize_thesis_question() writes a question sentence targeting that gap theme, then hands it directly to Module 1's real formalize()
  4. The returned ResearchQuestion is validated exactly the way Module 1 validates any question — every PICO slot filled, or MissingSlotsError

04The Code, Explained

def formalize_thesis_question(gap_theme, population, comparison):
    question_text = (
        f"Does {gap_theme.lower()} moderate the effect of remote work on productivity "
        f"among {population}, compared to {comparison}?"
    )
    return formalize(
        question_text,
        framework=Framework.PICO,
        slots={
            "population": population,
            "intervention": "remote work arrangements",
            "comparison": comparison,
            "outcome": f"productivity, as moderated by {gap_theme.lower()}",
        },
    )

formalize and Framework in that function signature are imported directly from 01_research_question_formalizer/formalizer.py — not reimplemented, not a lookalike. The literal function this course opened with.

05Limits / What You Cannot Do Yet

Can't do yet

  • Detect themes where sources genuinely contest each other, not just where coverage is thin — an earlier version of this module tried and it didn't work on real data (see DECISIONS.md)
  • Write your actual thesis question for you — the template produces a scaffold a real researcher still has to refine and justify
  • Judge whether a thin theme is thin because it's genuinely under-researched, or because it just isn't very important

Can do

  • Turn a real, computed coverage gap into a specific, checkable claim instead of a vague "more research is needed"
  • Produce a research question that's immediately, mechanically validated — not just written and hoped to be complete
  • Prove that Module 1's very first validation logic still works, unmodified, twenty-nine modules and two tracks later

07Brain Exercise

This module's demo data has a genuinely interesting disagreement in it — self-reported outcomes trend positive, objectively measured outcomes trend negative — but the funnel never mentions it. Why not, if it's real and interesting?
Because this module was honest about what it could actually detect reliably. That cross-theme divergence is real and worth writing about in an actual thesis — but automatically detecting it would require comparing sentiment across themes, not just counting sources within a theme, which is a harder problem this module's simple coverage-count mechanism was never built to solve (an early attempt at a "contested theme" detector, tried during development, didn't find it either — see DECISIONS.md). The funnel only claims the one thing it can actually prove: this specific theme has unusually thin coverage. A real thesis writer reading the synthesis matrix by eye would likely notice the divergence too, and should — that's real analytical work no mechanical count can substitute for.

08There Are No Dumb Questions

Is the generated research question actually good enough to put in a real thesis proposal?
As a starting draft, plausibly — as a final version, no. It's mechanically complete (every PICO slot filled, validated) but the specific phrasing, scope, and justification still need a real researcher's judgment. Think of it the way a course outline isn't a finished essay: it's correctly structured, and that's exactly as far as structure alone can take you.
Why does closing the loop with Module 1 matter, beyond being a nice symmetry?
Because it's real evidence for this whole course's premise: that building small, verified, reusable pieces one at a time adds up to something bigger than any single piece. Module 1 was never written with Module 30 in mind — it was written to formalize a systematic review's PICO question. That it works, unmodified, for a thesis research question too is proof the abstraction was genuinely sound, not just convenient for its original use.
What would I actually do next, as a beginner who just finished this module?
Build your own real synthesis matrix (Module 29) from your own real sources, run `identify_thin_themes()` on it, and see what it finds. If nothing looks thin, that's useful information too — it might mean your review question needs to narrow further before a gap becomes visible.
