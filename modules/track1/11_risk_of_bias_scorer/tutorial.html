Module 11 — Track 1: Core EngineRisk-of-Bias / Quality Appraisal Scorer

Prerequisites: Study Design Taxonomy & Evidence Hierarchy

00Before You Start

Read Prerequisite 4, specifically its warning box: "the pyramid is a simplification... don't treat design type alone as a final verdict on quality." This module is what actually checks quality, per-study, beyond just knowing its design type.

01The Big Analogy

The health inspector who doesn't just average the checklist A restaurant inspection has ten checkpoints. Nine are spotless. The tenth is "raw chicken stored directly above ready-to-eat lettuce." A health inspector who averaged all ten checkpoints would hand out a glowing 90% grade — and someone could get salmonella from a restaurant with a 90%. A real inspector doesn't average; a serious violation on even one critical checkpoint can outweigh nine clean ones.

This module's score_checklist() does average — deliberately, as a teaching simplification — and its own self-checks prove exactly the health-inspector problem: a study with a serious, specific flaw (no blinding, an RCT's raw-chicken-over-lettuce moment) still scores 0.80, "low risk," because four other domains were clean. That's not a bug. It's this module honestly demonstrating why the real Cochrane RoB2 tool refuses to average at all.

02What Happens, Step by Step

  1. Based on a study's design, the right Checklist is selected from CHECKLISTS_BY_DESIGN — an RCT and a qualitative study never share one
  2. A human answers each item "yes", "unclear", or "no"
  3. score_checklist() validates every item is answered with a valid value, weights each answer (1.0/0.5/0.0), and averages
  4. The average maps to a band: "low risk" (≥0.8), "some concerns" (≥0.5), or "high risk"
  5. The single worst-scoring item is always reported alongside the average — a direct, deliberate mitigation for the averaging weakness this module documents rather than hides

04The Code, Explained

weighted = [(item.key, _WEIGHTS[answers[item.key]]) for item in checklist.items]
proportion = sum(w for _k, w in weighted) / len(weighted)
band = "low risk" if proportion >= 0.8 else "some concerns" if proportion >= 0.5 else "high risk"
lowest_key = min(weighted, key=lambda kv: kv[1])[0]

Three lines produce the average and the band; the fourth line is the module's answer to its own weakness — lowest_key means a human reading the result is never limited to the number that can hide a serious problem.

05Limits / What You Cannot Do Yet

Can't do yet

  • Match the real Cochrane RoB2 or CASP checklists verbatim — these are simplified, condensed versions
  • Weight one domain as more critical than another automatically — averaging treats every item equally, which is exactly the demonstrated weakness
  • Answer the checklist itself — a human still has to read the study and judge each item

Can do

  • Apply a design-appropriate checklist consistently, not a one-size-fits-all judgment call
  • Produce a real, reproducible score from real, validated inputs
  • Honestly surface exactly where its own scoring method can mislead, with a concrete number proving it

07Brain Exercise

If this module already knows averaging can hide a serious flaw, why not just fix it — e.g. cap the band at "high risk" whenever any single item is "no"?
That would be a real, defensible design — and it's much closer to what Cochrane RoB2 actually does (worst-domain-driven overall judgment). This module deliberately keeps the simpler averaging approach instead, specifically so the weakness stays visible and demonstrable rather than quietly patched over. A teaching module that silently "fixes" the problem the moment it's discovered would rob the learner of seeing the failure mode happen — which is the whole point of Example 1's output. A real production tool should absolutely make the fix this question suggests.

08There Are No Dumb Questions

Why keep an admittedly weaker scoring method instead of just implementing the real Cochrane RoB2 algorithm?
The real algorithm's value comes specifically from its domain-by-domain signalling questions and structured overall-judgment rules — a substantial, detailed specification. Reproducing it faithfully is a legitimate, larger undertaking than this module's scope; showing why averaging is the wrong shortcut, with a real demonstrated failure, teaches the underlying lesson honestly without requiring that full reproduction.
Could `unclear` legitimately mean something different for different checklist items?
In real appraisal practice, yes — "unclear" on "was blinding used" might mean "not reported," while "unclear" on "was the design appropriate" might mean genuine reviewer uncertainty. This module treats every "unclear" as worth exactly 0.5 regardless of item, which is a real simplification worth knowing about if you extend this module.
What happens to a study whose design doesn't match any of the three checklists?
This module doesn't handle that case — `CHECKLISTS_BY_DESIGN` only has three entries. A real review encountering a design outside RCT/cohort/qualitative (a case-control study, say) would need a fourth checklist added, following the exact same pattern as the other three.
