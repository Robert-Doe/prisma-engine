Concept Cluster 02 — Deep Dive 1Where Meta-Analysis Came From, and Methods This Course Doesn't Implement

01A Short Real History

  • 1904 — Karl Pearson combined data across several typhoid vaccine studies, an early precursor to formal pooling, though not using modern methods.
  • 1930s — Ronald Fisher developed a method for combining p-values across independent tests, an early formal statistical tool for synthesizing multiple studies' results.
  • 1976 — Gene Glass coined the term "meta-analysis" specifically, applying it to psychotherapy outcome studies in educational/psychological research — the origin of the name this course's Module 17 implements.
  • 1993 — The Cochrane Collaboration formed, formalizing and standardizing the modern systematic-review-plus-meta-analysis methodology this entire course's Track 1 is built around (Cochrane Handbook terminology appears throughout Modules 7, 11, 16, 17, 19).

Multilevel / robust variance estimation modelsHandling multiple, non-independent effect sizes from the same study (e.g. several outcomes reported by one paper)Module 17 assumes one effect size per study; real reviews often need to handle this more complex, correlated-data case
Why inverse-variance weighting (what Module 17 actually implements) is still a reasonable default: it's the most widely taught, most broadly applicable method, works for both binary and continuous outcomes with the same underlying logic, and is exactly what the Cochrane Handbook presents first before introducing the more specialized alternatives above.
