Concept Cluster 01 — Deep Dive 2How Identity Matching Fails, and What It Costs

01Two Directions of Failure

False positive: over-merging

Two genuinely different records get treated as duplicates, and one is silently discarded. Module 6's tutorial demonstrates this is exactly why the fuzzy-matching threshold and author-overlap gate exist — a real, distinct study ("Remote Work and Employee Wellbeing") sitting too close in title-similarity space to another ("...Employee Productivity") must NOT be merged away.

False negative: under-merging

Two records of the same real paper survive as separate entries. Module 25's umbrella review shows the downstream cost concretely: a naive sum of "10 primary studies" across overlapping reviews was really 8 unique studies — every uncaught duplicate inflates how much independent evidence appears to exist.

02A Real Integrity Implication: Duplicate and Near-Duplicate Publication

This isn't only an algorithmic curiosity. Research integrity literature documents real cases of duplicate publication (the same study published more than once, sometimes in different venues or slightly reworded) and, more seriously, coordinated paper mills producing large volumes of formulaic, templated papers with just enough surface variation to evade naive duplicate detection. A systematic review that fails to catch duplicate or near-duplicate publications of the same underlying study risks double-counting that study's effect in a meta-analysis — silently giving one real result more statistical weight than it should have, which is precisely the mechanism Module 17's forest plot visualizes as "weight" in the first place. Robust deduplication is not just a data- hygiene nicety; it's part of what keeps a meta-analysis's pooled estimate honest.

03Performance Tradeoffs, Concretely

ApproachCostWhen it's the right call
Exact match only (DOI)O(1) per record (hash lookup)Whenever a reliable identifier exists — always try this first, as Module 6 does
Unblocked fuzzy match (Module 6's Pass 2)O(n²) comparisonsSmall survivor sets after exact matching already did most of the work — fine for hundreds of records, not tens of thousands
Blocked fuzzy matchO(n × average block size), much less than O(n²) with a well-chosen blocking keyAny real-scale systematic review — Cochrane-scale reviews routinely screen 10,000+ records
Embedding-based similarity + approximate nearest-neighbor search (e.g. locality-sensitive hashing)Higher per-record cost, but sub-linear search at scaleVery large corpora where even blocking isn't selective enough, or where paraphrase-level duplicates matter
The throughline: every step up this table trades implementation complexity for the ability to handle more records without the comparison cost exploding. Module 6 deliberately stops at the simplest tier that's still correct for this course's scale — see its own DECISIONS.md for why that's a stated, not accidental, choice.
