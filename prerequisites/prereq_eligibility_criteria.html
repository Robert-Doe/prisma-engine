Prerequisite 5 of 9Eligibility Criteria Logic

The Big Analogy A rigorous screening process works like a bouncer with a written checklist taped inside the door, decided before the club opened — not a bouncer who sizes up each person in the moment and decides on vibes. The list doesn't change depending on who's asking, and anyone can check it against who got in and who didn't.

01Inclusion vs. exclusion criteria

  • Inclusion criteria — properties a record must have to proceed (e.g. "population is adult knowledge workers")
  • Exclusion criteria — properties that disqualify a record even if it meets every inclusion criterion (e.g. "conference abstract with no full text available")

They're often two views of the same predicate, but keeping them separate matters for reporting: PRISMA requires you to log which specific criterion excluded each record, and "must include" vs. "excluded because" read differently to someone auditing your decisions later.

02Why criteria must be written down first (a priori)

If you decide criteria while you're already looking at results, you can unconsciously shape them to fit the answer you expected — a study that contradicts your hypothesis suddenly looks "not quite relevant enough." Pre-registering the criteria (writing them down, ideally in a public protocol, before screening starts) is what makes the eventual excluded list trustworthy rather than suspicious.

The failure mode: criteria written loosely enough that two reasonable people would classify the same study differently. "Recent studies only" is not a criterion — "published 2015 or later" is.

03Criteria as a predicate over record fields

Mechanically, eligibility criteria are just a boolean function over a record's fields, derived directly from your PICO/PEO/SPIDER slots (Prerequisite 1):

def is_eligible(record):
    return (
        record.population_matches("adult knowledge workers")
        and record.year >= 2015
        and record.study_design in {"RCT", "cohort", "cross-sectional"}
        and record.language == "English"
    )
