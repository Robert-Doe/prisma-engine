Prerequisite 6 of 9Effect Sizes & Statistical Primitives

The Big Analogy Ten studies report temperature in a mix of Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin. You can't average them until you convert everything into one unit. An effect size is that common unit for research findings — it converts "63% improved in the treatment group vs. 41% in control" and "mean score 7.2 vs 5.9" into numbers that can sit in the same column of a table and be combined.

01The main effect-size types

MeasureUsed when outcome is..."No effect" value
Odds Ratio (OR)binary, from a case-control study1.0
Risk Ratio / Relative Risk (RR)binary, from a cohort/RCT with known baseline risk1.0
Mean Difference (MD)continuous, same measurement scale across studies0
Standardized Mean Difference (SMD, e.g. Cohen's d)continuous, but studies used different scales/instruments0

Notice the pattern: ratio measures (OR, RR) center on 1 — "no difference" means the ratio of the two groups is 1:1. Difference measures (MD, SMD) center on 0 — "no difference" means literally zero gap. Which family a study reports depends on its design (Prerequisite 4).

02Confidence intervals

An effect size alone is a point estimate — a best guess from a limited sample. A 95% confidence interval expresses the range the true effect plausibly falls in, given the sample size and variability observed. Wider interval = less certain (usually a smaller study); narrower = more certain (usually a larger one).

The one thing to memorize: if a ratio measure's confidence interval spans across 1 (e.g. OR = 1.3, 95% CI [0.9, 1.8]), or a difference measure's interval spans across 0, the result is not statistically significant at that threshold — the data can't rule out "no effect at all."

03Why this matters mechanically

Every study you include has its own effect size and confidence interval computed independently. Pooling them into one combined estimate — the actual arithmetic of a meta-analysis — is only valid once they're all expressed in the same unit. Get the wrong effect-size formula for a study's design and every downstream number, including the pooled estimate and the forest plot, is silently wrong.
