Prerequisite 8 of 9Positioning vs. Evidence-Synthesis Review

The Big Analogy A coroner's inquest and a defense attorney's closing argument both work from the same pile of evidence, but they're doing different jobs. The inquest tries to objectively account for everything the evidence shows, pooled fairly, with no side to argue for. The closing argument is built to make one specific case — it doesn't need to cover every angle, but it absolutely cannot misrepresent the other side's evidence, because that's the fastest way to lose credibility with the jury.

01Two different jobs

Evidence-synthesis review (PRISMA-style)Positioning review (USENIX/OSDI/NSDI-style "Related Work")
GoalObjectively answer "what does the evidence show?"Justify "why does this specific new contribution matter?"
CoverageExhaustive — a comprehensive, reproducible search is the whole pointSelective — covers what's needed to establish the gap, not every tangential paper
Organizing structureBy theme or outcome, across all included studies equallyBy approach/technique, each cluster contrasted against the new contribution
Typical artifactEvidence table, forest plot, PRISMA diagramComparison matrix (systems × dimensions), gap statement

02Different failure modes

Evidence-synthesis review fails when...

  • The search missed a relevant subset of studies (incomplete coverage)
  • Study quality wasn't accounted for (garbage in, garbage out)
  • Publication bias skewed the pooled estimate

Positioning review fails when...

  • It misrepresents what a prior system actually does (strawmanning)
  • It misses a directly overlapping prior system entirely — the single fastest way to get rejected by a USENIX-style program committee
  • Its comparison table cherry-picks dimensions that flatter the new contribution

03Why this matters mechanically

The two genres don't just look different — they need different tooling. A systematic review's engine (Modules 1–20) is built to maximize and audit coverage: every record's fate is logged, nothing quietly disappears. A positioning review needs a tool built to maximize accurate comparison: correctly and verifiably characterizing each competitor along the dimensions that matter for the argument. Modules 27–28 reuse the search/aggregation machinery from Track 1 but replace PRISMA-style screening and synthesis with a comparison matrix and a gap-statement generator, precisely because the goal changed.
