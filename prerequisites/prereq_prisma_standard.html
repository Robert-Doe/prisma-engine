Prerequisite 7 of 9The PRISMA Reporting Standard

The Big Analogy PRISMA is a receipt drawer. Keep every receipt from a shopping trip and anyone — an auditor, your future self — can reconstruct exactly what you bought, what you almost bought and put back, and why. Without the receipts, all anyone has is your word that the total is right.

01What PRISMA is

PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses) is a reporting standard: a checklist of what a systematic review must document, plus a required flow diagram. It doesn't tell you how to do a review — it tells you what you must disclose about how you did it, so someone else can judge (and in principle reproduce) it.

02Why it exists

It grew out of a documented problem: reviews published in the 1990s varied wildly in how thoroughly they described their search and screening process, making it impossible to tell a rigorous synthesis from a cherry-picked one just by reading the paper. An earlier standard, QUOROM (2000), covered only trials; PRISMA (2009, revised 2020) broadened and formalized it into the standard most journals now require.

03The flow diagram stages

The part of PRISMA every module in this course actually implements is the flow diagram — a running count of records at each stage:

  1. Identification — total records found across all sources, before deduplication
  2. Deduplication — records removed as duplicates
  3. Screening — records excluded at title/abstract stage, with reasons
  4. Eligibility — records excluded at full-text stage, with reasons
  5. Included — what's left, going into synthesis
The core discipline PRISMA enforces: every record that doesn't make it to "included" must have a stated reason at a stated stage. "We looked at 400 papers and picked the 12 good ones" is not PRISMA-compliant reporting — it's a black box.

04Why this matters mechanically

PRISMA compliance isn't a document you write at the end — it's a property of how your pipeline tracks state throughout. Every stage that removes records (dedup, screening, eligibility) has to log a reason alongside the removal, in real time, or the flow diagram at the end is reconstructed from memory instead of from an actual audit trail.
