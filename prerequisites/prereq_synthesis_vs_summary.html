Prerequisite 9 of 9Synthesis vs. Summary

The Big Analogy A plate with a sliced apple, a sliced banana, and a sliced orange kept in three neat, separate piles is still just three fruits described one at a time — a summary. A fruit salad is the same three fruits, cut up and combined so that every bite has a bit of each. Synthesis is the fruit salad: the same underlying material, recombined so it's organized by idea instead of by source.

01What summary reads like

Smith (2020) found that remote workers reported higher job satisfaction.
Jones (2019) found no significant change in output per hour for remote workers.
Lee (2021) found remote work increased self-reported productivity but decreased
measured output on collaborative tasks.

This is organized by source — one paragraph per paper. It's easy to write and easy to read, and it is the single most common mistake in a first-draft thesis literature review, because it feels like it's doing the job. It isn't: the reader has to do the synthesis themselves, in their head, while reading.

02What synthesis reads like

Self-reported outcomes and objectively measured outcomes diverge across the
literature: while self-reported satisfaction and productivity consistently rise
under remote work (Smith, 2020; Lee, 2021), studies using measured output show
a more mixed picture — no change overall (Jones, 2019), and a specific decline
on collaborative tasks (Lee, 2021). This divergence suggests remote work's
effect may depend heavily on task type, a distinction few studies control for.

This is organized by theme — "self-report vs. measured outcomes" — and each source is cited only where it supports a specific claim about that theme. Notice Lee (2021) appears in both halves, because its findings split across the theme. That's only possible once you've stopped organizing by source.

03The mechanical tell

The test: delete every heading from your draft and read it straight through. If it reads like a stack of separate book reports you could reorder without losing anything, it's a summary. If reordering the paragraphs would break an argument that's building toward something, it's synthesis.

04Why this matters mechanically

You can't will yourself into writing synthesis directly — the natural drafting instinct is to process sources one at a time, which produces summary. The fix is a tool that forces reorganization before you write prose: a grid with sources as rows and themes as columns, filled in source-by-source, then read back out column-by-column. Reading down a column is reading synthesis-in-waiting; reading across a row is reading a summary.
